# Mobile apps and websites

The NHS App is available to users as [an iOS app](https://itunes.apple.com/us/app/nhs-app/id1388411277?ls=1&mt=8), [an Android app](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.nhs.online.nhsonline) and as [a website](https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-app/account/).

The website version of the NHS App is strategically important despite only being used by around 4% of users. This is because it can be accessed by people without a smartphone, people with older operating systems or anyone who does not want to install the NHS App on their device.

We maintain parity between the 3 apps and we do not prioritise one over the other. This is so that we can maintain a consistent offer to all users.

There are some exceptions to this rule:

- There are some natural differences in design and behaviour due to different operating systems and conventions.
- The website version of the app lacks push notifications because browser notifications are a dreadful experience.
- The release strategy for a new feature or change might mean releasing to one version of the app first, with a fast follow to other versions soon after.
