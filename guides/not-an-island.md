# The NHS App is not an island

The NHS App is not a standalone service – it is part of the NHS’s digital ecosystem. Along with the NHS website and 111 Online it is one of the most frequently used parts of that ecosystem, with the power to shape interactions between the NHS and the public. 

Your digital health service does not exist in isolation. Users will experience it alongside other digital, analogue and in-person NHS services.

Most people perceive the NHS as one organisation and the NHS App as one service.

This means that:

- the vision, objectives and roadmap for the NHS App all need to be aligned with the wider national strategy for the NHS as a whole
- teams delivering services in the NHS App need to consider how their services fit into that wider ecosystem
- all NHS App services have dependencies on other digital services, e.g. NHS login, PDS, NHS Notify
- we all have a duty to keep the NHS App and services available through it consistent and coherent.

In practice, this means:

1. Decisions about where a service is located in the NHS App will be made by the core NHS App team.
2. Your service must follow the NHS Service Standard and NHS design system to be in the NHS App.
3. You must understand your service's dependencies and plan for how they impact the running of your live service.
4. Exceptions to existing design patterns and standards will be heavily scrutinised.
