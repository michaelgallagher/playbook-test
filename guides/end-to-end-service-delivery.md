# End-to-end service delivery

This guide sets out what we expect when teams are designing, delivering, and running end-to-end services in the NHS App. The goal is not to ship disconnected features. It is to help people solve real problems across real journeys, and to keep improving the service once it is live.

## The standards that guide this work

The [NHS service standard](https://service-manual.nhs.uk/standards-and-technology/service-standard) is our north star for end-to-end service delivery. It is the main benchmark for what good looks like.

This sits alongside:

| Standard | What it means here |
| -------- | ------------------ |
| [NHS service standard](https://service-manual.nhs.uk/standards-and-technology/service-standard) | The main benchmark for how we design and assess services |
| [Technology Code of Practice](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/the-technology-code-of-practice) | The expectations for how we make technology and delivery decisions |
| [Engineering Redlines](https://nhs.sharepoint.com/sites/X26_EngineeringCOE/SitePages/Red-lines.aspx) | The hard, non-negotiable engineering baseline |
| [User experience principles](user-centred-design/user-experience-principles.md) | A description of what a good end product looks like in our context |
| [User experience metrics](user-centred-design/user-experience-metrics.md) | Notes on how we measure the effectiveness of services |

These do not compete with one another. The Service Standard sets the direction, the Technology Code of Practice and Engineering Redlines set important constraints, our user experience principles help teams make better day-to-day decisions, and our approach to measurement helps teams know whether their work is effective.


## Basic expectations

### Design with the whole journey in mind

Teams should understand the user's needs and full journey, including the steps that happen before, during, and after the part they directly own. This includes non-digital steps, local service variation, and touchpoints owned by other teams or organisations.

### Deliver as a multidisciplinary service team

Design, product, content, engineering, delivery, research, analytics, clinical, and operational considerations need to come together early. End-to-end service delivery is not something one discipline can add at the end.

### Run what you launch

Launching a service is not the end of the work. Teams are expected to monitor performance, respond to issues, learn from feedback, and keep improving the service over time. For more on service ownership, see [How to own an NHS App feature or service](owning-an-nhs-app-service.md).

### Be explicit about trade-offs and constraints

If scope, policy, technical dependencies, safety constraints, or organisational boundaries limit what can be done, say so plainly. Teams should explain what part of the problem is being solved now, what is not yet solved, and what needs to happen next.


## Aspirations and realities

We should be ambitious. The right target is still to solve the whole problem and create a joined-up, accessible and inclusive experience. In practice, this is not always completely possible.

The health system is complex. Services often span national and local organisations, legacy systems, funding boundaries, supplier contracts, and delivery dependencies that no single team can resolve on its own. Because of that, there will be times when we cannot remove every handoff, unify every pathway, or give users one completely seamless end-to-end journey.

That reality matters, but it is **not** an excuse to stop at the boundary of your own team, programme, or platform.

When the ideal is not yet possible, we still expect teams to:

- understand the whole problem
- reduce friction wherever they can
- make handoffs clear and humane
- avoid creating dead ends or false promises
- document gaps, dependencies, and risks
- keep working towards a more joined-up service over time

A partial improvement can still be worthwhile, but it should be an honest step towards the service standard, not a rationalisation for ignoring it.
